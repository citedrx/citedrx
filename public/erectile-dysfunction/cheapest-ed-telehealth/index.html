What this means in plain language

The cheapest and highest-scored provider are the same one here

Unlike some categories CitedRx covers, Hims is both the lowest-priced provider on this page and the highest-scored (9.1/10). That's unusual: evidence quality is roughly equal across FDA-approved generic providers in this category, so Hims's edge comes mainly from offering both brand and generic with transparent, flexible pricing, not from a lower price buying worse medicine.

Per-dose pricing can be deceptively low or high

A $2-4/dose price sounds cheap, but if you'd use it several times a week, a flat monthly plan like GoodRx Care's $18/mo could end up costing less overall. Do the math on your actual expected usage before assuming the lowest per-unit number is the lowest total cost.

Compounded isn't always cheaper, and it trades away evidence match

Rugiet, the most expensive provider on this list at $149/mo, is also compounded, meaning you're paying a premium for a product that isn't the one tested in PDE5 inhibitor trials. BlueChew is comparatively cheap but carries an FDA warning letter over how its compounded claims were marketed. Neither compounded provider on this page is cheap and evidence-matched at once.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest ED telehealth provider?

By published price, Hims's roughly $2/dose generic sildenafil is the lowest figure on this page, followed by Keeps ($3.20/dose) and Ro ($4/dose). But per-dose and per-month pricing aren't directly comparable: your actual monthly cost depends on how often you use it. Among flat monthly plans, GoodRx Care's $18/mo is the lowest.

Is the cheapest ED provider the best one?

Not automatically. Two of the lower-priced providers on this page, BlueChew and Rugiet, are compounded-primary, meaning the decades of PDE5 inhibitor trial evidence doesn't directly apply to what they prescribe, and BlueChew's parent company carries an FDA warning letter. Price is one input; see each provider's full CitedRx Score for the complete picture.

Why do per-dose and per-month prices appear on the same list?

Providers publish pricing differently: some bill per dose, others a flat monthly or quarterly subscription. This page sorts by the raw published number for comparability, but a per-dose price only tells you your actual monthly cost once you factor in how many doses you'd actually use.
