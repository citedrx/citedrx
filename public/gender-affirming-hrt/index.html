How we scored these

  1. Evidence Match (4 pts). Full credit for prescribing consistent with Endocrine Society and WPATH SOC8 guidance, including a clearly stated informed-consent or comparable evaluation process; partial credit where the process or guideline alignment isn't clearly documented.
  2. Independent Rating (2 pts). Scaled from the best available third-party rating; several providers in this category have thin BBB or review data, which we note rather than estimate.
  3. Insurance & Support (2 pts). Full credit for broad major-insurance acceptance; partial for self-pay/sliding-scale-only models, regardless of how reasonable the sliding scale is.
  4. Monitoring Rigor (1 pt). Full credit for a clearly stated commitment to Endocrine Society-aligned lab monitoring (baseline plus every 3 months in year one); partial where this isn't clearly documented by the provider.
  5. Regulatory Standing (1 pt). Full credit by default; deducted for documented, provider-specific complaint patterns (not state-law access restrictions outside the provider's control).

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a therapist's letter to start gender-affirming hormone therapy?

No, not necessarily. WPATH's Standards of Care Version 8 (2022) explicitly recognizes informed consent, where a prescribing clinician reviews your history and you consent to treatment after discussing risks and benefits, as a valid, legitimate model alongside the older mental-health-referral-letter approach. Most telehealth providers in this category, including every one ranked on this page, use an informed consent model.

Is gender-affirming hormone therapy safe?

The Endocrine Society's clinical practice guideline and WPATH's Standards of Care both treat it as safe, standard-of-care treatment for adults when properly monitored, with regular lab work (typically every 3 months during the first year). A 2025 telehealth-specific cohort study of 342 adults found significant reductions in depression and anxiety after starting hormone therapy. Like any hormone therapy, it carries real, individual risks that a clinician should review with you, including elevated hematocrit on testosterone therapy.

Does insurance cover gender-affirming hormone therapy?

It depends on the provider and your plan. Some telehealth providers, including FOLX Health, Transhealth, and Circle Medical, bill major insurance directly. Others, including Plume and QueerDoc, operate primarily as self-pay or sliding-scale, though you may still be able to use insurance for labs and prescriptions even if the visit itself isn't billed to insurance.

Is testosterone a controlled substance?

Yes. Testosterone used for masculinizing hormone therapy is a Schedule III controlled substance federally, the same classification as testosterone prescribed for TRT. Estradiol and anti-androgens used for feminizing hormone therapy are not controlled substances.

Can I get gender-affirming hormone therapy through telehealth in every state?

Not universally. A clinician generally must be licensed in the state where you're physically located at the time of the visit, and access varies by state and by provider. Florida in particular requires an in-person visit for adult hormone therapy, not just for minors, which is why some providers on this page note an in-person requirement specific to Florida patients. Confirm availability in your state directly before enrolling.
