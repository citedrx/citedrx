No human trial has shown NMN or NR extends human lifespan

That's the claim most longevity marketing overstates, and correcting it directly is the point of this page. NMN and NR are OTC supplements now cleared of regulatory ambiguity; sermorelin is a compounded, prescription-only peptide with a meaningfully thinner evidence base. We compare them separately — they're different purchase decisions, and forcing them into one ranking would hide that.

Frequently asked questions

Does NMN or NR extend human lifespan?

No human trial has demonstrated that NMN or NR extends human lifespan. The ~20% median lifespan increase sometimes cited comes from a 2024 study in genetically premature-aging mouse models, not normal aging or humans. Human trials have shown NMN is safe and reliably raises blood NAD+ levels — that's a real, proven effect, just a different claim than lifespan extension.

Is NMN legal to buy as a supplement?

Yes, as of September 29, 2025, when the FDA reversed its 2022 position that had excluded NMN from the dietary supplement definition, with confirming letters to individual suppliers issued in December 2025. NR (nicotinamide riboside) was never restricted and has been a lawful GRAS supplement since 2016.

Is sermorelin the same kind of product as NMN or NR?

No. NMN and NR are over-the-counter dietary supplements. Sermorelin is a compounded, prescription-only peptide with no current Phase III trial data — its only FDA approval (under the brand Geref) was voluntarily withdrawn in 2008. All current adult use is off-label and unapproved-compounded, a meaningfully thinner evidence base than NMN or NR.

Is sermorelin safe?

It is not a controlled substance and is not on the FDA's restricted-compounding list, but as an unapproved compounded product it has not gone through FDA safety/effectiveness review the way an approved drug has. As of January 2026, FDA guidance requires compounders to document why an FDA-approved alternative (such as tesamorelin) isn't clinically appropriate before compounding sermorelin.

What dose of NMN is safe?

Human trials have tested doses up to 1,250 mg/day for 4-10 weeks without serious adverse events, reliably raising blood NAD+ levels. Most commercial products are dosed well below this ceiling.
