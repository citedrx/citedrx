What this means in plain language

Cheapest and highest-scored point in opposite directions here

Elektra Health and Gennev, CitedRx's two highest-scored menopause providers (tied at 8.5/10), are also the two most expensive entries on this page. That's not a coincidence: both charge more upfront for a substantive consult or visit with a menopause-specialized clinician, which is part of what the Specialization factor in CitedRx's scoring rewards. A lower price elsewhere on this list often means a lighter-touch, more subscription-style model instead.

A consult price isn't a recurring monthly cost

Gennev's $199 and Elektra Health's $249 are one-time initial visit or consult prices, not what you'd pay every month going forward, and both accept insurance for eligible patients, which can lower the real cost significantly. Don't rule either out purely on sticker shock without checking your own coverage first.

The cheapest option on this list has the weakest independent rating

Evernow is the lowest-priced provider here, but it also carries the lowest independent rating in this comparison (2.0/5, albeit a small review sample) over credentialing and lab-access complaints. A low price is worth confirming against independent reviews before enrolling, not just the number itself.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest menopause / HRT telehealth provider?

By published price, Evernow's plans starting around $35/mo are the lowest on this page, followed by Alloy Women's Health's $49 one-time consult. But the two highest-scored providers, Elektra Health and Gennev (both 8.5/10), are also the two most expensive by consult price ($249 and $199), so cheapest and best-evidenced point in opposite directions in this category more than in most CitedRx covers.

Why are the highest-scored menopause providers also the most expensive?

Elektra Health and Gennev both charge for a substantive initial consult or visit with a menopause-specialized clinician (an OB-GYN at Gennev), which is reflected in a higher upfront price than subscription-style competitors. That specialization is also a meaningful part of why they score highest on CitedRx's methodology: the two aren't unrelated.

Is the cheapest menopause provider worth it?

It can be, but check what you're giving up. Evernow's low rating in independent reviews (small sample) cites credentialing and lab-access complaints, and lower-priced options generally offer less menopause-specific clinical specialization than Elektra Health or Gennev. Price is one input; see each provider's full CitedRx Score for the complete picture.
