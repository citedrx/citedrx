BlueChew Review: Cost, Legitimacy, and a 2025 FDA Warning Letter

BlueChew sells compounded, chewable sildenafil and tadalafil, plus a higher-tier "GOLD" formula. It scores 4.7/10, and its parent company received an FDA warning letter in September 2025 over how it marketed those products.

The FDA warning letter, specifically

On September 9, 2025, the FDA sent a warning letter to Dermacare LLC, doing business as BlueChew, after reviewing the company's website. The FDA's concern: BlueChew's marketing described its compounded chewable tablets as containing the same active ingredients as FDA-approved drugs such as Viagra and Cialis. The FDA treats that framing as misleading, because a compounded product, custom-mixed by a pharmacy to order, is not FDA-approved or reviewed for safety and effectiveness the way the branded drug it's compared to actually was. The letter called for corrections to that marketing language.

This is a materially different, and less severe, concern than the drug-interaction warnings we flag for some other compounded ED products: it's about how the product was marketed, not a documented combination safety risk. But it's a real, dated regulatory record worth knowing before you compare BlueChew's claims to an FDA-approved alternative.

What's actually in the chew

BlueChew's standard tiers are compounded chewable tablets containing a single active ingredient, either sildenafil (commonly cited around 30–45mg) or tadalafil (commonly cited around 6–9mg), reportedly at various strengths. Its higher-tier "GOLD" formula is described in BlueChew's own marketing as adding apomorphine and oxytocin, additional compounds with different mechanisms than a PDE5 inhibitor. Because these are compounded products, none of them are the exact formulation tested in the pivotal sildenafil and tadalafil trials covered in our research library; dosing and inactive-ingredient details can also vary by batch in ways a standardized, FDA-approved tablet does not.

The genuine tradeoffs

What's genuinely offered

  • Chewable format may suit people who prefer not to swallow a pill, with faster buccal absorption claimed
  • Flexible pause-or-cancel subscription, a real, commonly cited convenience feature
  • Licensed telehealth prescribing process, not an unregulated supplement

The real tradeoffs

  • Compounded, not FDA-approved: none of BlueChew's lineup is the standardized, trial-tested tablet form
  • FDA warning letter (September 9, 2025) over marketing claims comparing its compounded chews to FDA-approved drugs
  • No plain FDA-approved option offered, unlike most higher-scored providers CitedRx compares in this category
  • Inconsistent pricing figures across sources, including BlueChew's own materials
  • GOLD tier adds untested ingredient combinations beyond a single PDE5 inhibitor

Frequently asked questions

Is BlueChew legit?

BlueChew is a real, prescription-only telehealth service operated by Dermacare LLC, not a scam. It scores 4.7/10 on CitedRx's methodology, below the FDA-approved-tier providers in this category, because its chewable tablets are compounded rather than FDA-approved, and the FDA sent its parent company a warning letter in September 2025 over marketing claims.

What did the FDA warning letter about BlueChew say?

The FDA sent a warning letter to Dermacare LLC, doing business as BlueChew, dated September 9, 2025, after reviewing the company's website. The FDA found that BlueChew's marketing described its compounded chewable tablets as containing the same active ingredients as FDA-approved drugs like Viagra and Cialis, a claim the FDA considered misleading since compounded products are not themselves FDA-approved or reviewed for safety and effectiveness the way those brand-name drugs are.

Is BlueChew FDA-approved?

No. BlueChew's chewable tablets are compounded by a pharmacy, meaning they are custom-mixed to order rather than manufactured and reviewed under the FDA's standard drug approval process. This is true of its standard sildenafil and tadalafil chews and its higher-tier GOLD formula, which reportedly adds apomorphine and oxytocin.

How much does BlueChew cost?

Reported pricing varies by source and plan, generally in the range of $20 to $30 per month depending on tablet count and billing frequency, or roughly $2.65 to $3.33 per chew. Confirm current pricing directly on BlueChew's site, since third-party figures are inconsistent.
