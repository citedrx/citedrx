The safety concern, specifically

DirectMax's formula reportedly combines sildenafil and tadalafil, two different PDE5 inhibitors, with apomorphine, a third compound with a different mechanism, in a single sublingual tablet. This isn't a minor technicality: FDA-approved sildenafil prescribing information includes a specific section addressing combination with other PDE5 inhibitors, stating that the safety and effectiveness of such combinations have not been adequately studied, that the combination may further lower blood pressure, and directing patients taking sildenafil not to also take other PDE5 inhibitors. Tadalafil's own labeling carries comparable guidance.

This matters because the PDE5 inhibitor trial evidence covered in our research library, including a 2024 meta-analysis of PDE5 inhibitor cardiovascular safety covering more than 1.2 million men, was built on single-agent use. None of that evidence transfers to an untested triple-ingredient combination, and the specific combination here runs counter to the FDA's own labeling guidance for one of its component drugs.

A promotional content pattern worth flagging

In researching this review, nearly every search result about DirectMax was a third-party "review" site, several using templated, SEO-optimized formats (press-release-style distribution, "Reviews 2026" titles, limited-time-discount framing) rather than genuine independent testing or reporting. Pricing claims varied wildly between these sites, from about $1.50 to $10.78 per dose, with no single source we could treat as authoritative. This pattern, a product marketed primarily through a dense network of affiliate-style content rather than verifiable independent coverage, is itself a reason for caution, separate from the formulation concern above.

The genuine tradeoffs

What's genuinely offered

  • Sublingual (under-the-tongue) delivery, a real pharmacological approach some patients prefer to swallowing a pill
  • A licensed, prescription-only compounding pharmacy dispenses the product, not an unregulated supplement
  • Single product bundles multiple active ingredients into one dose, for patients specifically seeking that

The real tradeoffs

  • Combines two PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil and tadalafil), a combination FDA-approved sildenafil labeling specifically advises against
  • Compounded and not FDA-reviewed: no clinical trial data exists on this specific three-ingredient combination
  • No plain, single-ingredient FDA-approved option offered, unlike most other providers CitedRx compares in this category
  • Inconsistent pricing across sources and urgency-framed promotional discounting, making the real cost hard to pin down
  • No verifiable independent rating found; most online coverage reads as affiliate or sponsored content

Frequently asked questions

Is DirectMax legit?

DirectMax is a real, prescription-only product from DirectMeds, dispensed by a licensed compounding pharmacy, not an outright scam. But it scores 2.5/10 on CitedRx's methodology, the lowest of any ED provider we compare, because of a real formulation safety concern and inconsistent, promotionally framed pricing, not because it's fraudulent.

Is it safe to combine sildenafil and tadalafil?

FDA-approved sildenafil labeling specifically addresses this: it states that combining sildenafil with another PDE5 inhibitor, such as tadalafil, has not been adequately studied, may further lower blood pressure, and advises patients taking sildenafil not to also take other PDE5 inhibitors. DirectMax's formula combines exactly that combination, plus apomorphine, in one compounded tablet.

How much does DirectMax cost?

Reported pricing is inconsistent across sources, ranging from roughly $7.45 to $10.78 per dose depending on strength and where you look, with promotional offers like a "limited-time 33% off" discount advertised for new patients. We could not verify a single authoritative current price; confirm directly on DirectMeds' site before enrolling, and be skeptical of urgency-framed discount offers.

Why does DirectMax score so low?

Three factors compound: it's a compounded, not FDA-approved, triple-ingredient combination with no clinical trial data on that specific formulation; it combines two PDE5 inhibitors against FDA labeling guidance for sildenafil; and the online "review" content about it is dominated by what read as affiliate or sponsored marketing rather than independent assessment, making its real-world safety and value genuinely hard to verify.

DirectMax vs Hims: what's the difference?

Hims prescribes plain FDA-approved generic or brand-name sildenafil or tadalafil individually, the exact products tested in PDE5 inhibitor trials, and scores 9.1/10. DirectMax combines two PDE5 inhibitors plus apomorphine in one compounded tablet, a combination FDA labeling advises against, and scores 2.5/10.
