DirectMax vs. Hims: Is Combining Two PDE5 Inhibitors Worth It?

DirectMax combines sildenafil and tadalafil, two PDE5 inhibitors, in one compounded tablet, against FDA labeling guidance for sildenafil. Hims prescribes either drug individually, the exact products behind the clinical trial evidence. Here's the factor-by-factor difference.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · See all 10 providers →
The short answer: Hims scores far higher than DirectMax (9.1 vs 2.5) and typically costs far less, since Hims's roughly $2/dose generic sildenafil or tadalafil is a fraction of DirectMax's reported $7.45–10.78/dose. DirectMax combines two PDE5 inhibitors plus apomorphine in one compounded tablet; FDA-approved sildenafil labeling specifically advises against combining it with another PDE5 inhibitor. DirectMax is not a confirmed CitedRx referral partner, and this comparison wasn't paid for or reviewed by either company.

What this means in plain language

Combining two PDE5 inhibitors isn't a bonus feature, it's a labeled warning

FDA-approved sildenafil prescribing information includes a specific section on combination with other PDE5 inhibitors: it states the safety and effectiveness of combining sildenafil with another PDE5 inhibitor like tadalafil have not been adequately studied, that the combination may further lower blood pressure, and advises against it. DirectMax's formula is exactly that combination, plus a third compound. Hims prescribes sildenafil or tadalafil individually, matching how each drug was actually studied in the trials covered in our research library.

More ingredients doesn't mean more evidence

DirectMax's three-compound combination has no clinical trial data of its own; it's assembled by a compounding pharmacy rather than reviewed by the FDA as a fixed combination product. Hims's plain generic sildenafil or tadalafil is the directly trial-tested drug, at a small fraction of DirectMax's reported per-dose cost.

Verify pricing yourself before assuming either number is final

Hims publishes clear, consistent pricing. DirectMax's reported per-dose cost varies substantially depending on the source, with promotional "limited-time" discount framing common in the content we found about it. Confirm current pricing directly on each provider's site, and treat urgency-framed discounts with skepticism.

Frequently asked questions

Is DirectMax worth it compared to Hims?

On CitedRx's methodology, no: DirectMax scores 2.5/10, the lowest of any ED provider we compare, against Hims's 9.1/10. DirectMax combines two PDE5 inhibitors, sildenafil and tadalafil, in one compounded tablet, a combination FDA-approved sildenafil labeling specifically advises against. Hims prescribes either drug individually, the exact products tested in PDE5 inhibitor clinical trials, at a fraction of the cost.

Why does combining sildenafil and tadalafil matter?

FDA-approved sildenafil labeling states that combining it with another PDE5 inhibitor, such as tadalafil, has not been adequately studied, may further lower blood pressure, and advises against the combination. DirectMax's formula does exactly that, plus adds a third compound, apomorphine, that Hims's plain products do not contain.

Which costs more, DirectMax or Hims?

Reported DirectMax pricing is inconsistent across sources, roughly $7.45 to $10.78 per dose. Hims's plain generic sildenafil or tadalafil typically runs close to $2 per dose. Hims is both the lower-cost and the higher-scored option on CitedRx's methodology.

Does DirectMax offer a plain FDA-approved option?

No. DirectMax's product is a compounded, multi-ingredient combination; it does not offer a plain, single-ingredient FDA-approved sildenafil or tadalafil option the way Hims does.
