The safety concern, specifically

OakMax combines sildenafil and tadalafil, two different PDE5 inhibitors, in a single compounded tablet designed to dissolve in the mouth rather than being swallowed whole. This is the same category of concern we flagged with DirectMax: FDA-approved sildenafil prescribing information includes a specific section addressing combination with other PDE5 inhibitors, stating that the safety and effectiveness of such combinations have not been adequately studied, that the combination may further lower blood pressure, and directing patients taking sildenafil not to also take other PDE5 inhibitors. Tadalafil's own labeling carries comparable guidance.

This matters because the PDE5 inhibitor trial evidence covered in our research library, including a 2024 meta-analysis of PDE5 inhibitor cardiovascular safety covering more than 1.2 million men, was built on single-agent use. None of that evidence transfers to an untested two-drug combination that runs counter to the FDA's own labeling guidance for one of its component drugs.

Oak also sells the plain, FDA-approved-equivalent option

Unlike some compounded-only providers we compare, Oak's ED page also lists plain generic sildenafil, plain generic tadalafil, and a daily low-dose tadalafil option, the same FDA-approved-equivalent products that score well for providers like Hims and GoodRx Care. This review doesn't cover those: it's scoped specifically to OakMax, the compounded combination product, because that's the program Oak actively promotes through its affiliate network under its own name. If you're considering Oak for ED treatment, ask specifically which product you'd be prescribed, since the plain single-agent options on the same storefront don't carry the combination concern above.

Frequently asked questions

Is OakMax legit?

OakMax is a real, prescription-only compounded product sold through Oak Longevity (oaklovesyou.com), not an outright scam. It scores 2.8/10 on CitedRx's methodology, the second-lowest of any ED provider we compare, because it combines two PDE5 inhibitors against FDA labeling guidance and has no independent rating specific to the product.

Is it safe to combine sildenafil and tadalafil?

FDA-approved sildenafil labeling specifically addresses this: it states that combining sildenafil with another PDE5 inhibitor, such as tadalafil, has not been adequately studied, may further lower blood pressure, and advises patients taking sildenafil not to also take other PDE5 inhibitors. OakMax's dissolve-in-mouth tablet combines exactly that pair.

How much does OakMax cost?

We could not independently confirm current OakMax pricing. Oak's own ED page was not reachable through our research tools this round, and no third-party source covers OakMax pricing specifically. Confirm current pricing directly on Oak's site before enrolling.

Does Oak offer anything besides OakMax for ED?

Yes, and this review doesn't cover those options. Oak's ED page also lists plain generic sildenafil, plain generic tadalafil, and a daily low-dose tadalafil option, the same FDA-approved-equivalent products most higher-scored providers in this comparison prescribe. This review is scoped specifically to OakMax, the compounded combination product, because that's the program actively promoted through Oak's affiliate network.

OakMax vs DirectMax: what's the difference?

Both combine PDE5 inhibitors against FDA labeling guidance, the core reason both score lowest in this comparison. DirectMax adds a third compound, apomorphine, on top of sildenafil and tadalafil; OakMax combines just the two. That's a meaningfully smaller formulation than DirectMax's, but it doesn't change the underlying guidance violation, which is why OakMax (2.8/10) scores only slightly above DirectMax (2.5/10), not meaningfully higher.
