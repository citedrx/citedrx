QueerMed vs. FOLX Health: CitedRx's Top Two GAHT Providers Compared

QueerMed (8.6/10) and FOLX Health (7.9/10) are ranked 1st and 2nd of 6 providers, and both follow WPATH SOC8 informed-consent guidance: neither is the obvious "worse" choice on evidence. Here's exactly where the 0.7-point gap comes from, and where FOLX actually wins.

The short answer: QueerMed edges out FOLX Health (8.6 vs 7.9) mainly on nationwide reach and a named, credentialed founder with a trans-specialty practice dating to 2017, not on guideline alignment: both follow WPATH SOC8 informed-consent care. FOLX Health's edge is broader major-insurance acceptance (Aetna, BCBS, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare) and a published flat cash-pay rate ($59/mo), versus QueerMed's sliding-scale self-pay model.

What this means in plain language

The gap is about history and reach, not guideline alignment

Both providers prescribe according to WPATH SOC8 and Endocrine Society guidance covered in our evidence deep-dive, so the clinical evidence applies equally to either. QueerMed's edge comes from being a longer-established, nationwide trans-specialty practice with a named physician founder, a meaningful trust signal in a category with many newer entrants.

If insurance billing matters most, FOLX Health is the stronger pick

FOLX Health bills major insurance directly, which can make it meaningfully cheaper than either provider's cash-pay rate if you're covered by one of its accepted plans. QueerMed's sliding scale and financial-aid fund are the better path if you're uninsured or underinsured rather than trying to use existing coverage.

Florida patients: check the in-person requirement either way

FOLX Health explicitly notes an in-person visit requirement for Florida patients, a state-law requirement rather than a FOLX policy choice. Confirm current requirements for your state directly with either provider before enrolling, since access rules shift.

Frequently asked questions

Is QueerMed better than FOLX Health?

By CitedRx's scoring, yes: QueerMed scores 8.6/10 versus FOLX Health's 7.9/10. The gap mainly reflects QueerMed's nationwide reach and its trans-specialty practice dating to 2017 with a named founder, not a difference in guideline alignment: both follow WPATH SOC8 informed-consent care.

Which is cheaper, QueerMed or FOLX Health?

FOLX Health publishes a flat cash-pay rate starting around $59/month; QueerMed uses sliding-scale self-pay without a single published number, though it offers financial aid through the Point of Pride HRT Access Fund. For insured patients, FOLX's direct billing with major carriers (Aetna, BCBS, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare) may end up cheaper than either cash-pay option.

Does QueerMed or FOLX Health accept insurance?

FOLX Health accepts major insurance directly, including Aetna, BCBS, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, and some Medicaid plans. QueerMed is primarily self-pay with a sliding scale, though labs and prescriptions may still be insurance-eligible even when the visit itself isn't billed to insurance.

Is FOLX Health available in every state?

Mostly, but Florida patients require an in-person visit, a state-law requirement rather than a FOLX policy choice. QueerMed is available nationwide without that specific carve-out, though both require a clinician licensed in the state where you're physically located.
