Alloy vs. Winona: Why the Higher-Rated Provider Scores Lower

Winona has the best independent customer rating of any menopause provider CitedRx compares (4.6/5, 6,200+ reviews), yet Alloy Women's Health scores higher overall (7.2/10 vs 6.8/10). Here's exactly why, factor by factor.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · See all 9 providers →
The short answer: Alloy scores higher on CitedRx's overall methodology (7.2/10 vs 6.8/10) despite Winona having the better independent customer rating (4.6/5 vs 4.3/5, and nearly 3x the review count). CitedRx's score weights evidence-based prescribing and menopause specialization alongside customer satisfaction, not customer rating in isolation, and Alloy's lower one-time price ($49 vs Winona's recurring $89/mo) is a separate factor entirely.

What this means in plain language

A higher customer rating doesn't automatically win

Winona's 4.6/5 across 6,200+ reviews is the strongest independent rating of any menopause provider in CitedRx's comparison, better than Alloy's 4.3/5. But CitedRx's overall score also weights prescribing approach and menopause-specific specialization (see our WHI evidence deep-dive for why that nuance matters), which is where Alloy pulls ahead enough to offset Winona's rating edge.

The price models aren't really comparable as single numbers

Alloy's $49 is a one-time consult fee; Winona's $89 is billed monthly. Over several months of ongoing care, Alloy is meaningfully cheaper in total, but confirm what's actually included in each price (follow-ups, medication, labs) before treating either number as the full cost.

Neither is exclusively FDA-approved

Both Alloy and Winona are categorized as "Mixed" on CitedRx's prescribing scale, meaning FDA-approved hormone therapy is available but isn't the exclusive offering. If prescribing that avoids compounded products entirely matters most to you, Elektra Health and Gennev, CitedRx's two FDA-Approved-First top scorers, are worth comparing instead.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, Alloy or Winona?

On CitedRx's overall methodology, Alloy scores higher (7.2/10 vs 6.8/10), even though Winona has the better independent customer rating (4.6/5 vs 4.3/5, and more reviews). CitedRx's score weights evidence-based prescribing and specialization alongside independent rating, not customer satisfaction alone.

Which is cheaper, Alloy or Winona?

Alloy's $49 is a one-time consult fee; Winona's $89 is a recurring monthly cost. Over several months, Alloy is substantially cheaper, though the two aren't pricing the exact same thing: confirm what's included in each before comparing directly.

Which has the better independent customer rating, Alloy or Winona?

Winona, at 4.6/5 across more than 6,200 reviews, versus Alloy's 4.3/5 across more than 2,100 reviews. Winona has the highest independent customer rating of any menopause provider CitedRx compares.

Do Alloy and Winona prescribe the same way?

Both are categorized as Mixed on CitedRx's prescribing-approach scale, meaning FDA-approved hormone therapy is available but isn't exclusively what's prescribed. Alloy specifically notes a compounded testosterone option used when needed; ask either provider directly what you'd actually be prescribed.
