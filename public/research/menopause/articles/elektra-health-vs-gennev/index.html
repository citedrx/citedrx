Elektra Health vs. Gennev: CitedRx's Top Two Menopause Providers Compared

Elektra Health and Gennev tie at 8.5/10, CitedRx's two highest-scored menopause/HRT providers. Neither is the obvious "worse" choice: here's exactly where each one actually has the edge.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · See all 9 providers →
The short answer: Elektra Health and Gennev tie at 8.5/10 with genuinely different strengths rather than one leading the other. Elektra Health is evidence-first by design, explicitly avoiding compounded bioidentical pellets, with 1:1 coaching and, as of 2026, a $0 HRT option in 11 states through its Oscar Health "HelloMeno" ACA plan. Gennev's network is exclusively board-certified OB-GYNs and already accepts major insurance (Aetna, Anthem, UnitedHealthcare) directly, at a slightly lower self-pay starting price ($199 vs $249).

What this means in plain language

A tied score, but for different reasons

Elektra Health's score reflects an explicitly evidence-first approach, no compounded bioidentical pellets, plus included 1:1 coaching the WHI reanalysis and NAMS guidance in our research library applies most directly to. Gennev's score reflects its clinician specialization (OB-GYN only) combined with already-broad major-insurer acceptance. Neither factor makes one "better": they're different strengths that happen to land at the same number.

Elektra Health's 2026 insurance plan is a genuine development worth checking

If you're in Arizona, Florida, Georgia, Iowa, Missouri, Nebraska, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Tennessee, or Texas, Elektra Health's new HelloMeno ACA marketplace plan (launched with Oscar Health) can bring HRT down to $0, along with no-cost primary care, gynecology, and labs. Outside those states or that plan, expect the $249 cash-pay consult price instead.

Gennev is the lower self-pay price today, with insurance already in place

If you're not eligible for Elektra's ACA plan and want a provider with major insurance acceptance already built in rather than an "expanding" network, Gennev's $199 self-pay visit and its Aetna/Anthem/UnitedHealthcare coverage is the more immediately accessible option for many patients right now.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, Elektra Health or Gennev?

They tie at 8.5/10 on CitedRx's methodology, with different strengths rather than one being strictly better. Elektra Health leads on evidence-first prescribing that explicitly avoids compounded bioidentical pellets, plus 1:1 coaching. Gennev leads on clinician specialization (an exclusively board-certified OB-GYN network) and already accepts major insurance (Aetna, Anthem, UnitedHealthcare) directly.

Which is cheaper, Elektra Health or Gennev?

Gennev's $199 self-pay initial visit is slightly cheaper than Elektra Health's $249 cash-pay initial consult. Both accept insurance for eligible patients, which can lower the real cost significantly below either cash-pay number.

Does Elektra Health or Gennev take insurance?

Both do, to varying degrees. Gennev is covered by major insurers including Aetna, Anthem, and UnitedHealthcare directly. Elektra Health's payer network is expanding (nearly 40 partners) and, as of 2026, offers a $0 HRT option for eligible patients through its Oscar Health "HelloMeno" ACA marketplace plan in 11 states.

What is Elektra Health's HelloMeno plan?

HelloMeno is a 2026 ACA marketplace health plan Elektra Health launched with Oscar Health, available in Arizona, Florida, Georgia, Iowa, Missouri, Nebraska, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Tennessee, and Texas. It includes no-cost hormone replacement therapy, primary care, gynecology, behavioral health visits, and labs for enrolled members.
