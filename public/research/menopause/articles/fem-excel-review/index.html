Fem Excel Review: Cost, Legitimacy, and What They Actually Prescribe

Fem Excel is the women's health division of Excel Medical, prescribing compounded bioidentical hormones on a self-pay membership model. Here's what the evidence covers, what it costs, and a real customer complaint worth knowing before you enroll.

Last reviewed: November 2026 · CitedRx Score: 4.8/10 · See all 10 providers →
The short answer: Fem Excel scores 4.8/10 on CitedRx's methodology, our lowest score in this category. What the trial evidence actually covers

According to the company, Fem Excel's bioidentical hormone prescriptions are compounded by licensed U.S. pharmacies based on individual prescriptions. That's a meaningfully different product from the small set of FDA-approved bioidentical hormone formulations that exist (there are only 13, per a National Academies review of compounded hormone therapy), and it's a different category from the standardized estradiol and progesterone products most higher-scored providers in this comparison default to. As we explain on our WHI evidence deep-dive, the reinterpreted WHI evidence and current NAMS/ACOG guidance apply most directly to FDA-approved formulations; a compounded product carries the same regulatory caveat we apply to every compounded provider across every category we cover, not something specific to Fem Excel.

What customers actually say

Fem Excel holds roughly a 4.5/5 TrustScore across hundreds of reviews on Trustpilot as of November 2026, a genuinely strong number worth weighing against the specific complaints below.

What reviewers praise

  • Clinicians described consistently as helpful, caring, and knowledgeable
  • Ongoing, personalized care rather than a one-time prescription and done

What reviewers complain about

  • Cost is the most common complaint; the company has responded that its pricing reflects an ongoing personalized-care model, not a one-time treatment
  • At least one reviewer reported being pushed to schedule a follow-up before labs and prescriptions had actually arrived
  • At least one reviewer alleged being charged repeatedly after cancellation; the company replied with an apology rather than disputing the charge

Based on CitedRx's review of Fem Excel's Trustpilot profile as of November 2026; the rating and mix of reviews shown can change. Frequently asked questions

Is Fem Excel legit?

Fem Excel is a real telehealth platform, the women's health division of Excel Medical (sister brand to Male Excel), with licensed clinicians and a genuine Trustpilot presence (around 4.5/5 across hundreds of reviews). It scores 4.8/10 on CitedRx's methodology, our lowest menopause score, mainly because its hormone prescriptions are compounded rather than FDA-approved, it's self-pay only, and we found a documented complaint about being charged after cancellation.

How much does Fem Excel cost?

Fem Excel's own published comparison puts its all-inclusive membership at roughly $195-295 per month depending on visits, tests, and medications, with bioidentical hormones averaging an additional $95-165 per month. These are the company's own figures from a marketing comparison page, not independently verified; confirm current pricing directly before enrolling.

Does Fem Excel prescribe FDA-approved hormones?

No, not as its standard offering. According to the company, Fem Excel's bioidentical hormone prescriptions are compounded by licensed U.S. pharmacies based on individual prescriptions, not the small set of FDA-approved bioidentical hormone products that exist. We found no indication Fem Excel offers a standardized FDA-approved option alongside the compounded one.

Does Fem Excel accept insurance?

No. Fem Excel operates as a self-pay membership model; insurance, including Medicare, is not accepted. This is a meaningful gap compared to higher-scored providers like Gennev and Elektra Health, which accept major insurance.

Is LegitScript certification confirmed for Fem Excel?

Fem Excel's own outreach describes itself as LegitScript-certified, but we could not independently confirm this through LegitScript's public certification lookup or any other independent source. We're not treating it as verified fact, and recommend checking LegitScript's own certification database directly if this matters to your decision.
