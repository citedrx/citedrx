Brightside Health Review: Cost, Legitimacy, and Outcomes

Brightside Health is CitedRx's top-ranked mental health telehealth provider, using measurement-based SSRI/SNRI prescribing and publishing its own outcomes data. Here's what the evidence actually covers, what it costs, and where it's genuinely not the right fit.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · CitedRx Score: 9.1/10 · See all 7 providers →
The short answer: Brightside Health scores 9.1/10 on CitedRx's methodology, ranked 1st of 7 mental health telehealth providers. It uses PHQ-9/GAD-7 symptom tracking plus a proprietary algorithm to guide SSRI/SNRI prescribing, and publishes its own outcomes data, though that data is company-reported, not independently verified. What the evidence actually covers

Brightside prescribes SSRI/SNRI antidepressants, the drug class covered in our SSRI evidence deep-dive (including the Cipriani meta-analysis and the STAR*D controversy), guided by structured symptom-scale tracking (PHQ-9 for depression, GAD-7 for anxiety) rather than undifferentiated prescribing. That measurement-based approach is a meaningfully stronger evidence signal than most competitors in this category offer.

A real caveat: Brightside's most-cited outcomes figures, such as roughly 71-72% of clients reporting clinically significant improvement, come from company-published or company-funded studies, not independent randomized controlled trials. Read them as encouraging self-reported data rather than definitive independent proof, a distinction that applies to most telehealth-reported outcomes in this category, not just Brightside's.

The genuine tradeoffs

What's genuinely good

  • Measurement-based prescribing using validated symptom scales (PHQ-9, GAD-7), not just a one-time intake
  • Publishes its own outcomes data, which is more transparency than many competitors offer, even if not independently verified
  • Psychiatry and therapy available separately or combined, with broad insurance acceptance
  • Lowest entry price among self-pay flat-rate providers in this comparison ($95/mo psychiatry-only)

The real tradeoffs

  • Outcomes data is company-reported, not from independent randomized controlled trials: read the percentages as encouraging, not definitive
  • Combined psychiatry plus therapy ($349/mo) costs more than the cheapest insurance-first competitors if you're in-network elsewhere
  • Scoped to SSRI/SNRI medication and standard talk therapy only, not a fit if you need ADHD or other controlled-substance prescribing

Frequently asked questions

Is Brightside Health legit?

Yes. Brightside Health uses a measurement-based model (PHQ-9/GAD-7 symptom tracking plus a proprietary algorithm) to guide SSRI/SNRI prescribing, and publishes its own outcomes data. It scores 9.1/10 on CitedRx's own evidence-based methodology, currently ranked 1st of 7 mental health providers we compare.

How much does Brightside Health cost?

Psychiatry (medication management) only runs about $95/month, therapy-only is $299/month, and combined psychiatry plus therapy is $349/month. Confirm current pricing and your specific insurance coverage directly with Brightside before enrolling.

Are Brightside Health's outcomes data independently verified?

No, and this is worth knowing. Brightside's cited outcomes (such as roughly 71-72% of clients reporting clinically significant improvement) come from the company's own published or company-funded studies, not independent randomized controlled trials. That doesn't mean the figures are false, but they should be read as encouraging self-reported data, not definitive independent proof.

Does Brightside Health accept insurance?

Brightside accepts broad insurance for many plans alongside its self-pay rates. Confirm your specific plan's coverage directly, since acceptance and out-of-pocket cost vary by insurer.

Brightside Health vs Rula: what's the difference?

Brightside emphasizes measurement-based psychiatric care with published outcomes tracking; Rula emphasizes the lowest average copay through an insurance-first, in-network model. See our full Brightside vs. Rula comparison for a factor-by-factor breakdown.
