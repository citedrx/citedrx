Brightside Health vs. Rula: CitedRx's Top Two Mental Health Providers Compared

Brightside Health (9.1/10) and Rula (8.0/10) are ranked 1st and 2nd of 7 providers, both scoped to SSRI/SNRI medication and standard talk therapy. Here's exactly where the 1.1-point gap comes from, and where Rula actually wins.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · See all 7 providers →
The short answer: Brightside edges out Rula (9.1 vs 8.0) mainly on evidence signal: measurement-based symptom tracking (PHQ-9/GAD-7) plus published outcomes data versus Rula's large in-network provider pool without comparable outcomes reporting. Rula's edge is cost for insured patients: an average copay around $15/session, among the lowest in this category, versus Brightside's $95/mo self-pay psychiatry-only rate.

What this means in plain language

The gap is about measurement, not medication

Both providers prescribe within the SSRI/SNRI scope covered in our SSRI evidence deep-dive, so the drug-class evidence applies equally to either. Brightside's edge comes from using validated symptom scales to track and adjust treatment, plus publishing its own outcomes (company-reported, not independently verified), which Rula doesn't offer as a comparable feature.

If you're insured and in-network, Rula is likely cheaper

Rula's roughly $15 average copay is built specifically around in-network insurance billing. If your plan is in-network with Rula, it will typically beat Brightside's self-pay rate. If you're uninsured, underinsured, or Rula isn't in-network with your plan, Brightside's flat, predictable self-pay pricing may be the more realistic comparison.

Neither is a fit for ADHD or stimulant prescribing

Both providers deliberately stay within SSRI/SNRI and talk-therapy scope, which is why both are included in CitedRx's comparison in the first place (see who we excluded and why on the full comparison page). Neither is the right choice if you specifically need ADHD medication management.

Frequently asked questions

Is Brightside Health better than Rula?

By CitedRx's scoring, yes: Brightside scores 9.1/10 versus Rula's 8.0/10, mainly because Brightside's measurement-based symptom tracking and published outcomes data are a stronger evidence signal than Rula's model. Rula's advantage is cost: its insurance-first design produces one of the lowest average copays in this category.

Which is cheaper, Brightside or Rula?

For insured patients in-network with Rula, its roughly $15 average copay per session is typically cheaper than Brightside's $95/mo psychiatry-only self-pay rate. If you're not in-network with Rula, Brightside's flat self-pay pricing may end up more predictable and comparable in total cost.

Do Brightside and Rula prescribe the same way?

Both are scoped to SSRI/SNRI medication and standard talk therapy, not ADHD or stimulant prescribing. Brightside differentiates on measurement-based tracking (PHQ-9/GAD-7 plus a proprietary algorithm); Rula's model centers on a large in-network credentialed provider pool rather than published outcomes data.

Does Rula accept insurance?

Yes, Rula is built around in-network insurance billing, which is the core of its lowest-average-copay positioning. Brightside also accepts insurance for many plans alongside self-pay rates; confirm your specific plan's coverage with either provider.
