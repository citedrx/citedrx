Talkiatry vs. Klarity: Why CitedRx Scores One and Not the Other

Talkiatry and Klarity Health both offer online psychiatry, but they aren't comparable the way most providers on this site are. Talkiatry is scored (6.6/10, the lowest of the 7 we rank). Klarity Health isn't scored at all, and this page explains exactly why.

Last reviewed: November 2026 · See all 7 scored providers →
The short answer: Talkiatry is an in-network, SSRI/SNRI-focused psychiatry platform that CitedRx scores at 6.6/10, the lowest of the 7 mental-health providers we rank, mainly due to a sharply polarized Trustpilot rating (2.5/5, 948 reviews) with recurring billing and cancellation complaints. Klarity Health isn't scored by CitedRx at all: its core model centers on same-day ADHD stimulant prescribing, a category we exclude entirely due to documented complaint patterns and a looming DEA regulatory deadline, the same treatment we give Cerebral and Done.

What this means in plain language

This isn't a close call between two similar providers

Most comparisons on this site are between two providers that both made our cut, differing on price or specialization. This one is different: Talkiatry stays within the SSRI/SNRI and non-stimulant scope covered in our evidence deep-dive, while Klarity Health's core offering, same-day ADHD stimulant access, puts it in the same excluded category as Cerebral and Done, both of which faced federal investigations over stimulant-prescribing practices.

Talkiatry's low score is still a real, earned score

Being scored at all, even at the bottom of our table, means Talkiatry's prescribing model is within the evidence-based scope we cover. Its 6.6/10 reflects genuine, documented concerns: a Trustpilot rating of 2.5/5 across 948 reviews, 66% of them one-star, with recurring complaints about unexpected charges and cancellation fees. Five-star reviews consistently praise the psychiatrists themselves, so the complaint pattern appears concentrated in billing and administration, not clinical care.

Klarity's exclusion is about the model, and a deadline is coming

BBB complaint records describe patients being charged for conditions that weren't discussed and matched with providers not licensed to prescribe the specific medication advertised, on top of an independent report finding Klarity's own blog, credentialing materials, and patient contracts disagreeing on fees, refunds, and start dates. Separately, current DEA flexibilities allowing remote stimulant prescribing without a prior in-person exam are scheduled to end December 31, 2026, a deadline that bears directly on Klarity's entire prescribing model.

Frequently asked questions

Is Talkiatry better than Klarity Health?

They're not directly comparable on CitedRx's methodology: Talkiatry scores 6.6/10 as an in-network, SSRI/SNRI-focused psychiatry platform. Klarity Health isn't scored at all, because its core model centers on same-day ADHD stimulant prescribing, a category we exclude from this vertical entirely due to documented complaint patterns and an upcoming DEA regulatory deadline, not because we ranked it low.

Why doesn't CitedRx score Klarity Health?

Klarity Health markets same-day ADHD stimulant prescriptions, and BBB complaint records include reports of patients being charged for conditions not discussed and matched with providers not licensed to prescribe the specific medication advertised. A separate, independent report also found Klarity's own blog, credentialing page, and patient contracts disagreeing with each other on fees, refunds, and start dates. We exclude this category of provider from scoring entirely, the same treatment we give Cerebral and Done for similar reasons.

Is Talkiatry legit?

Yes, with real caveats worth knowing. Talkiatry is an in-network psychiatry platform accepting hundreds of insurance plans including Medicare, and it's included in CitedRx's scored comparison at 6.6/10, the lowest of the 7 providers we score. Its Trustpilot rating (2.5/5 across 948 reviews) is sharply polarized: billing and cancellation complaints dominate the one-star reviews, while five-star reviews consistently praise the psychiatrists themselves.

What is the DEA telehealth deadline and why does it matter here?

Current DEA flexibilities that allow clinicians to prescribe controlled substances, including ADHD stimulants, via telehealth without a prior in-person exam are scheduled to end December 31, 2026. This directly affects platforms built around remote stimulant prescribing, a category that includes Klarity Health, and may force significant changes to how those platforms operate going forward.
