Henry Meds vs. TrimRx: Nearly Identical Price, Very Different Score

Henry Meds ($179/mo) and TrimRx ($174/mo) are priced within $5 of each other, but CitedRx scores them 2.3/10 and 5.0/10: a more than two-fold gap. The price tag alone won't tell you that. Here's what actually separates them.

Last reviewed: September 2026 · See all 15 providers →
The short answer: TrimRx scores more than double Henry Meds (5.0 vs 2.3) for two concrete reasons, not vibes: TrimRx offers real brand-name GLP-1s (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound) alongside compounded options, while Henry Meds is compounded-only, and Henry Meds carries an active, unresolved litigation deduction (Eli Lilly, over compounded-tirzepatide marketing) that TrimRx doesn't have. The sticker prices are nearly identical; the underlying product and regulatory picture are not.

What this means in plain language

Compounded-only vs. a real branded option

TrimRx's menu includes actual FDA-approved Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound, not just compounded versions of the same molecules. That means the STEP and SURMOUNT trial evidence in our research library applies directly to what you'd be prescribed if you choose TrimRx's branded tier. Henry Meds doesn't offer that branded path at all: everything is compounded, which is why it scores lowest on Evidence Match regardless of price.

The litigation is a real, disclosed factor, not a dealbreaker by itself

Eli Lilly's litigation against Henry Meds over compounded-tirzepatide marketing is civil and ongoing, not a finding of wrongdoing, and plenty of compounded-GLP-1 providers operate without this specific issue. But it's a real, checkable fact relevant to evaluating the company, and CitedRx's regulatory-standing factor exists specifically to surface things like this rather than treat every compounded provider as interchangeable.

Price alone genuinely doesn't predict the gap here

At $174–179/mo, these two providers are priced within rounding error of each other. If you were shopping on price alone, you'd have no signal that one offers a real branded option and a clean regulatory record while the other doesn't. That's exactly the kind of gap a price-sorted list can hide and a factor-by-factor comparison surfaces.

Frequently asked questions

Why does TrimRx score so much higher than Henry Meds if they cost almost the same?

Two reasons. First, TrimRx offers brand-name GLP-1s (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound) alongside compounded options, while Henry Meds is compounded-only, which scores lower on CitedRx's Evidence Match factor since no major GLP-1 trial tested a compounded formulation. Second, Henry Meds carries an active, unresolved litigation deduction (Eli Lilly, over compounded-tirzepatide marketing) that TrimRx doesn't have.

Is Henry Meds legitimate despite the litigation?

The litigation is civil and ongoing, not a finding of wrongdoing, and doesn't mean Henry Meds is operating illegally today. It's a real, disclosed regulatory factor relevant to evaluating the company, which is why CitedRx reflects it in the score rather than ignoring it.

Does TrimRx or Henry Meds offer an oral option?

TrimRx offers a compounded oral option in addition to injectable; Henry Meds is injectable only. Neither offers the FDA-approved Wegovy pill, which is limited to branded-primary providers.
