Oak Weight Loss Review: Cost, Legitimacy, and a Pricing-Consistency Problem

Oak is a telehealth platform prescribing compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide through a named medical group. Here's what the evidence covers, what it costs, and a real inconsistency on Oak's own site worth knowing before you enroll.

What the trial evidence actually covers

Oak's core product is compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide: the same active ingredients as Ozempic/Wegovy and Mounjaro/Zepbound, but prepared by a compounding pharmacy rather than the original manufacturer. As we explain on our full evidence page, no published trial, including STEP and SURMOUNT, has tested the compounded version specifically, so that trial data doesn't transfer automatically to what Oak prescribes. Some third-party coverage describes a separate branded, insurance-coordinated path at Oak alongside the compounded one; we could not independently confirm that offering from Oak's own materials, so ask directly which tier you'd actually be prescribed.

A real pricing-consistency problem, not just third-party noise

Most of the newer, lower-scored providers CitedRx compares have thin data simply because little has been published about them. Oak is different: multiple independent reviewers report that Oak's own pricing pages disagree with each other, listing $119/mo on one page, $167/mo on another, and $190/mo on a third. That's not conflicting third-party speculation, it's a documented internal inconsistency, and it's the single biggest factor behind Oak's score. Get a specific, current, written quote directly from Oak before enrolling, and don't treat any single number, including the ones on this page, as final.

The genuine tradeoffs

  • Prescriptions issued through a named medical group (NVP Medical Group, P.A.), not an anonymous prescribing pool
