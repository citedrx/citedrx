Oak vs. Ro: Compounded Access or Branded Evidence?

Oak (3.2/10) and Ro (9.3/10) sit far apart on CitedRx's methodology. Oak is a current CitedRx referral partner; this comparison is just as honest about where it falls short as any other provider we cover.

Last reviewed: November 2026 · See all 21 providers →
The short answer: Ro scores far higher than Oak (9.3 vs 3.2) because it prescribes FDA-approved branded GLP-1s almost exclusively, the exact products tested in the STEP and SURMOUNT trials, while Oak's core offering is compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide. Oak's one potential edge is price, but multiple independent reviewers report that Oak's own pricing pages disagree with each other, which is itself a real mark against it. What this means in plain language

The evidence-match gap is the whole story here

Ro's menu is predominantly FDA-approved branded product, so the STEP and SURMOUNT trial data applies directly to what most Ro patients are prescribed. Oak's compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide aren't the formulations those trials tested, which is the single largest driver of the score gap between the two.

A lower headline price isn't a clean advantage here

Oak's lowest reported figure, $119/mo, would undercut Ro's ongoing $149/mo rate if it held up. But independent reviewers have found $119, $167, and $190 per month each listed on different Oak pages at different times, which makes "Oak is cheaper" a claim you'd need to verify yourself, not a fact to take at face value.

Oak isn't an anonymous operation, even with a low score

Oak's prescriptions are issued through a named medical group, and it has a genuine, checkable Trustpilot presence, real signals of legitimacy that some lower-scored providers in this category lack entirely. A low CitedRx Score reflects the evidence tier and pricing clarity, not a claim that Oak is a scam.

Frequently asked questions

Is Oak better than Ro?

No, not on CitedRx's methodology: Ro scores 9.3/10 against Oak's 3.2/10. Ro prescribes FDA-approved branded GLP-1s almost exclusively, the exact products tested in the STEP and SURMOUNT trials. Oak's core offering is compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide, and multiple reviewers report inconsistent pricing across Oak's own site.

Is Oak cheaper than Ro?

On paper, Oak's lowest reported figure ($119/mo) undercuts Ro's ongoing $149/mo rate, but Oak's own pricing pages disagree with each other ($119, $167, and $190/mo have each appeared on different Oak pages), so it's not a clean comparison. Get a specific, current quote from Oak before assuming it's the cheaper option.

Does Oak offer FDA-approved GLP-1 medication like Ro?

Not confirmed. Ro's menu is predominantly FDA-approved branded product (Wegovy, Zepbound, Ozempic). Oak's core offering is compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide; some third-party coverage describes a separate branded path at Oak, but we could not independently confirm it.

Why does CitedRx carry a review of Oak if it scores so low?

Oak is a current CitedRx referral partner, and we review every partner with the same methodology and the same willingness to say where it falls short as any other provider. A low score doesn't disqualify a provider from being listed; it's the point of scoring independently of payment.
