Ro Review: Cost, Legitimacy, and What They Actually Prescribe

Ro is CitedRx's top-ranked GLP-1 telehealth provider, predominantly branded with a compounded semaglutide option and a dedicated prior-authorization team. Here's what the evidence covers, what it costs, and where it's genuinely not the right fit.

Last reviewed: November 2026 · CitedRx Score: 9.3/10 · See all 21 providers →
The short answer: Ro scores 9.3/10 on CitedRx's methodology, ranked 1st of 21 GLP-1 telehealth providers, with a 4.4/5 U.S. News rating. It prescribes predominantly FDA-approved branded GLP-1s (Wegovy, Zepbound, Ozempic), including the Wegovy oral pill, with a dedicated prior-authorization team for insured members. Membership is $39 for the first month, then $149/mo, with medication billed separately. It's not a sponsored partner: this review and its ranking weren't paid for or reviewed by Ro.

What the trial evidence actually covers

Ro's menu is predominantly FDA-approved branded product: semaglutide (Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Zepbound), at the doses tested in STEP and SURMOUNT. That evidence, including STEP 1's 14.9% average weight loss and SURMOUNT-1's 20.9%, applies directly to what most Ro patients are actually prescribed, which is the main reason it scores highest on CitedRx's Evidence Match factor among the 21 providers we compare.

Ro also offers compounded semaglutide as a self-pay option. As we explain in detail on our full evidence page, no published trial has tested the compounded version specifically, so that trial data doesn't transfer automatically to that option. Ask which one you'd actually be prescribed before enrolling.

Results: what to actually expect

Ro's own marketing cites 15-20% average body weight loss over 12 months, a claim that lines up reasonably well with STEP 1's 14.9% average (semaglutide) and SURMOUNT-1's 20.9% average (tirzepatide), the two trials behind Wegovy and Zepbound. That's an average, not a guarantee: both trials dosed patients over roughly 4 months of escalation before reaching a maintenance dose, with weight loss continuing to build through 6-12+ months, and individual results varied meaningfully around that average in both trials. If you're not seeing meaningful change after a few months at maintenance dose, that's a reasonable point to raise a dose adjustment or medication switch with your prescriber, not necessarily a sign the approach has failed.

The genuine tradeoffs

What's genuinely good

  • Broadest branded medication selection of any provider we track, including both Wegovy and Zepbound
  • Offers the Wegovy oral pill, not just injectable, for patients who prefer it
  • Dedicated prior-authorization team for insured members
  • 4.4/5 U.S. News rating, among the highest-rated providers we compare
  • Compounded semaglutide option for patients who want it

The real tradeoffs

  • Membership and medication are billed separately, and neither branded nor compounded medication pricing is published as a single headline number: your actual monthly cost depends on insurance and which drug you're prescribed
  • No coaching or behavior-change program highlighted, unlike Found or Noom Med
  • The compounded semaglutide option carries the same evidence caveat as any compounded provider: it's not the trial-tested product

What customers actually say

Ro holds roughly a 3.9/5 TrustScore ("Great") across nearly 5,900 Trustpilot reviews as of November 2026, one of the larger independent review bases of any provider in this comparison.

What reviewers praise

  • A fast evaluation and prescribing process, with several reviewers reporting medication shipped within a few days of signing up
  • Responsive messaging with prescribers and coaching staff

What reviewers complain about

  • Pricing clarity is the most common complaint: some reviewers describe the initial quote or sales framing as misleading relative to what they were ultimately billed
  • Billing confusion tied to insurance coordination, and occasional unanswered questions before enrollment

Frequently asked questions

Is Ro legit?

Yes. Ro is one of the largest U.S. telehealth platforms, prescribing predominantly FDA-approved branded GLP-1s (Wegovy, Zepbound, Ozempic) with a dedicated prior-authorization team for insured members. It holds a 4.4/5 rating on U.S. News's telehealth comparison and scores 9.3/10 on CitedRx's own evidence-based methodology, currently ranked 1st of 21 providers we compare.

How much does Ro cost?

Ro's membership is $39 for the first month, then $149/month, with medication billed separately from the membership fee. Medication pricing depends heavily on your insurance coverage and which drug you're prescribed; confirm your specific price directly with Ro before enrolling.

Does Ro accept insurance?

Ro includes a dedicated prior-authorization team for commercially insured members seeking coverage for branded medication. Confirm your specific plan's coverage directly, since GLP-1 coverage varies widely by insurer.

What does Ro prescribe?

Predominantly FDA-approved branded GLP-1s: Wegovy and Zepbound, available as a weekly injection or, for Wegovy, a daily oral pill, plus Ozempic. Ro also offers compounded semaglutide for patients who don't have insurance coverage or prefer that option.

Ro vs Found: what's the difference?

Ro prescribes predominantly branded GLP-1s, which is why it scores higher (9.3 vs 8.3) on CitedRx's evidence-match factor specifically. Found offers more of a mixed branded-and-compounded menu with included coaching at a lower starting price. See our full Found vs. Ro comparison for a factor-by-factor breakdown.

How soon will I see results on Ro?

Based on the underlying trial timelines, not a Ro-specific guarantee: the STEP and SURMOUNT trials dosed patients over roughly 4 months of escalation before reaching a maintenance dose, with meaningful average weight loss continuing to build over 6-12+ months. Ro's own marketing cites 15-20% body weight loss over 12 months, which is broadly consistent with STEP 1's 14.9% average and SURMOUNT-1's 20.9% average, but individual results vary and some patients see slower or faster change.

What if a GLP-1 from Ro doesn't work for me or causes side effects?

Clinical trials show most, not all, patients respond meaningfully to a given GLP-1, and side effects (mainly gastrointestinal) are common, especially during dose escalation. If a medication isn't working or side effects are hard to tolerate, your prescribing clinician can typically adjust your dose or switch you to a different GLP-1 within your existing plan; ask about this directly rather than assuming you're locked into the first prescription you receive.

Can I switch from Ro to a different provider?

Yes, you're not contractually locked into one GLP-1 telehealth provider. Switching generally means canceling your Ro membership and completing a new intake elsewhere, and a gap of several days to a few weeks during the transition for the new provider's intake and shipping is common. Confirm Ro's current cancellation process and refund terms for any unused membership period directly in your account before switching, since those terms can change.
