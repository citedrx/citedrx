Ro vs. Mochi Health: Is the Lower Price Worth the Score Gap?

Ro (9.3/10) and Mochi Health (6.5/10) sit far apart on CitedRx's methodology. Mochi Health's draw is a lower published price and a quarterly-monitoring model; here's exactly what that trade costs you in evidence match and support.

Last reviewed: October 2026 · See all 15 providers →
The short answer: Ro scores far higher than Mochi Health (9.3 vs 6.5) mainly because Ro's menu is predominantly FDA-approved branded product, while Mochi Health's medication tier is mixed, leaning on compounded semaglutide. Ro also offers a dedicated prior-authorization team and a published independent rating (4.4/5 on U.S. News); we found no comparable independent rating published for Mochi Health. Mochi Health's appeal is its lower headline price and quarterly clinical check-in model. Neither Ro nor Mochi Health is a confirmed CitedRx referral partner.

What this means in plain language

The evidence-match gap is the biggest single factor

Ro's branded-primary menu means the STEP, SURMOUNT, and SELECT trial data applies directly to what most Ro patients are prescribed. Mochi Health's mixed tier, leaning on compounded semaglutide, means that evidence doesn't transfer the same way for many of its patients. This is the single largest driver of the score gap, not a judgment on legitimacy.

A lower headline price isn't always the full cost

Mochi Health's published pricing (around $99/mo for medication plus a separate membership fee) can look cheaper than Ro's $149/mo membership, but third-party reviews have flagged the two-part pricing structure as harder to total up than a single number suggests, with lab work often billed separately. Get Mochi Health's full current cost breakdown directly before assuming it's the cheaper option.

Less frequent monitoring isn't automatically worse, but ask about it

Mochi Health markets a quarterly check-in cadence as part of its lower-cost model. That may be entirely appropriate for a stable patient, but it's a genuinely different support structure than more frequent touch points other providers offer, and worth asking about directly rather than assuming either model fits your situation.

Frequently asked questions

Is Ro better than Mochi Health?

On CitedRx's methodology, yes: Ro scores 9.3/10 against Mochi Health's 6.5/10, driven mainly by Ro's predominantly branded-medication menu, its dedicated prior-authorization team, and an independent rating Mochi Health doesn't have a comparable published figure for. Mochi Health's draw is a lower published price point and quarterly clinical monitoring.

Is Mochi Health cheaper than Ro?

On paper, closer than the score gap might suggest: Mochi Health lists compounded semaglutide around $99/mo plus a separate membership fee, while Ro runs $39 for the first month then $149/mo membership, with medication billed separately on both. Mochi Health's total cost structure has been criticized as harder to pin down than a single headline number suggests, so get a full breakdown directly before comparing.

Does Mochi Health offer brand-name GLP-1s?

Mochi Health's medication tier is mixed, offering compounded semaglutide and, depending on eligibility, other options, rather than the predominantly branded menu Ro prescribes. This is the main driver of the evidence-match gap between the two providers' scores.

What does Mochi Health's quarterly monitoring actually mean?

Mochi Health markets quarterly clinical check-ins as part of its lower-price model, versus the more frequent touch points some higher-priced providers offer. Whether that monitoring cadence is sufficient for you is a conversation to have directly with a prescriber, not something CitedRx's scoring methodology weighs on its own.
