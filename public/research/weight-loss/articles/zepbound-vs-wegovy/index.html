Zepbound vs. Wegovy: What the Trials Actually Show

Zepbound (tirzepatide) and Wegovy (semaglutide) are the two FDA-approved injectable GLP-1s specifically indicated for chronic weight management. Each has its own pivotal trial showing strong results, but they've never been tested directly against each other for weight loss. Here's what each trial actually measured, and where the real tradeoffs are.

Last reviewed: September 2026 · Full trial evidence →
The short answer: Zepbound produced greater average weight loss in its own trial than Wegovy did in its own trial (SURMOUNT-1: −20.9% at 72 weeks vs. STEP 1: −14.9% at 68 weeks), but that's a comparison across two separate trials with somewhat different populations, not a head-to-head result. Wegovy's unique advantage is SELECT, a published cardiovascular-outcomes trial showing a 20% reduction in heart attack, stroke, and cardiovascular death: Zepbound doesn't have that data yet. Zepbound's unique advantage is an FDA approval for obstructive sleep apnea, which Wegovy doesn't carry.

What this means in plain language

The weight-loss gap is real, but it's not a head-to-head result

Tirzepatide's dual GLP-1/GIP mechanism is the leading explanation for why Zepbound's trial showed a larger average loss than Wegovy's. But SURMOUNT-1 and STEP 1 are two separate trials, run in different years with somewhat different enrolled populations. The closest thing to a direct comparison, SURPASS-2, tested tirzepatide against semaglutide at a lower 1mg dose in people with type 2 diabetes, not the 2.4mg Wegovy dose in the general weight-loss population that most people asking this question actually mean. No trial has directly compared the two at their approved weight-loss doses.

Wegovy has published heart-health data that Zepbound doesn't have yet

SELECT is the only completed cardiovascular-outcomes trial in this drug class, and it's specific to semaglutide: a 20% relative reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events across more than 17,000 participants. Eli Lilly has cardiovascular-outcomes trials for tirzepatide underway, but as of this page's last review, none have reported results. If cardiovascular risk reduction specifically (not just weight loss) is your priority, that published evidence currently exists only for semaglutide.

Zepbound has an approval Wegovy doesn't: sleep apnea

SURMOUNT-OSA, a 469-person trial, found tirzepatide reduced apnea-hypopnea events by roughly 55% relative to placebo in adults with obesity and moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea, which led to an FDA approval specific to that condition. Wegovy doesn't carry a sleep-apnea indication.

Frequently asked questions

Is Zepbound stronger than Wegovy?

In separate trials, yes on average: Zepbound's pivotal SURMOUNT-1 trial showed −20.9% body weight at 72 weeks on the 15mg dose, versus Wegovy's STEP 1 trial showing −14.9% at 68 weeks. But "stronger" from two separate trials in different (though similar) populations is not the same as a head-to-head result, and individual response varies.

Has Zepbound been compared directly to Wegovy in a clinical trial?

Not for weight loss specifically. The closest head-to-head data is SURPASS-2, which compared tirzepatide to semaglutide 1mg in people with type 2 diabetes, not the 2.4mg dose approved as Wegovy, and not in the general weight-loss population. As of this page's last review, no published trial has directly compared tirzepatide to semaglutide 2.4mg for weight loss in a non-diabetic population.

Does Wegovy or Zepbound have better heart-health data?

Wegovy has a unique advantage here: SELECT, a 17,604-person cardiovascular-outcomes trial, found semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 20%. Zepbound does not yet have a published cardiovascular-outcomes trial of its own.

Does Zepbound have an advantage Wegovy doesn't?

Zepbound is also FDA-approved for obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity, based on the SURMOUNT-OSA trial, which found a roughly 55% relative reduction in apnea events versus placebo. Wegovy does not carry a sleep-apnea indication.

Can I switch from Wegovy to Zepbound, or vice versa?

Generally yes, with your prescriber's guidance: both are GLP-1-class injectables with established dose-titration schedules, and providers routinely switch patients between them for cost, insurance, tolerability, or effectiveness reasons. There isn't published trial data on switching outcomes specifically, so treat any expected results as individual, not guaranteed.
