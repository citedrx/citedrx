What the GLP-1 clinical trials actually show

Every major randomized controlled trial behind semaglutide and tirzepatide for weight loss, plus the newest FDA-approved oral pills and the 2026 pipeline (retatrutide, CagriSema), synthesized in one place, so you can see what's proven, what's still early, and how that should shape which provider and medication you choose.

Last reviewed: September 2026 · 12 approved-drug trials, 34,000+ trial participants · See the provider rankings → · Articles →
The short answer: across placebo-controlled trials, tirzepatide (Zepbound/Mounjaro) produced the largest average weight loss studied: up to 20.9% of body weight at 72 weeks in SURMOUNT-1, while semaglutide (Wegovy/Ozempic) has the deepest evidence base overall, including the only published GLP-1 cardiovascular-outcomes trial (SELECT), which showed a 20% reduction in heart attack, stroke, and cardiovascular death.

The 2026 pipeline: what's approved, and what isn't

The short answer: two new oral GLP-1 pills are already FDA-approved and prescribable in 2026: orforglipron (brand name Foundayo, approved April 1, 2026) and oral semaglutide 25mg (the Wegovy pill, approved December 2025). Retatrutide and CagriSema, the two drugs generating the most search interest as "the newest weight loss drug," are not approved and are not legally available for purchase in any form, including compounded, as of this page's last review.

Retatrutide: the biggest number, and the biggest gap between "trial result" and "available drug"

TRIUMPH-1's 28.3% average weight loss at the 12mg dose (up to 30.3% in participants with a higher baseline BMI) is a genuinely large result, larger than any approved GLP-1's published figure. But retatrutide has never been FDA-approved in any form, and federal law explicitly bars it from being compounded: the FDA said so directly and sent warning letters in September 2025 to sites selling "compounded retatrutide" or "retatrutide peptide" online. Anything sold under that name outside a clinical trial is not a regulated medication, regardless of what the packaging claims. Eli Lilly has indicated a Q1 2027 filing target, which puts realistic approval in late 2027 to 2028, not 2026.

CagriSema is closer to approval, but didn't beat Zepbound head-to-head

CagriSema's own placebo-controlled trial (REDEFINE 1) is strong: 22.7% average weight loss vs. 2.3% on placebo, and it beat each of its individual components (semaglutide alone, cagrilintide alone) on their own. Novo Nordisk filed for FDA approval in December 2025, with a decision plausible in late 2026 or early 2027. But in a separate head-to-head trial against Lilly's already-approved Zepbound, CagriSema underperformed: about 20% average weight loss vs. 24% for Zepbound over 84 weeks. It is not currently approved or available.

The two pills that actually exist solve a real access problem: needles

Orforglipron (Foundayo) and the oral Wegovy pill are both already approved, already prescribable, and both come as a once-daily tablet rather than a weekly injection: a meaningful difference for people who want GLP-1 therapy but are needle-averse, or for whom cold-chain injectable storage is a real barrier. Their weight-loss results (11.2% and 16.6% respectively, at the doses shown above) are real but more modest than the top injectable doses of semaglutide or tirzepatide, which is the tradeoff worth knowing before assuming "pill" automatically means "as effective."

What this means in plain language

Tirzepatide vs. semaglutide isn't just "which loses more weight"

Tirzepatide's dual GLP-1/GIP mechanism is associated with larger average weight reduction than semaglutide in the SURPASS-2 head-to-head trial, but individual response varies a lot, and semaglutide has a longer track record, including cardiovascular-outcomes data (SELECT) that tirzepatide doesn't yet have published at the same scale. "Which drug loses more weight on average" and "which drug has the most evidence behind it" are two different questions with two different answers.

What happens when you stop is a documented trial finding, not speculation

STEP 4 randomized people who'd already lost weight on semaglutide into either continuing the drug or switching to placebo. The group that stopped regained 6.9% of their body weight over the next 28 weeks, while the group that continued lost a further 7.9%. Most provider marketing omits this: it's a real finding from a real trial, and it should factor into how you think about committing to treatment, not just starting it.

SELECT and SUSTAIN-6 matter beyond the scale

Weight loss headlines get the attention, but SELECT and SUSTAIN-6 are about something else: whether the drug reduces heart attacks, strokes, and cardiovascular death. SELECT is the more notable of the two because it enrolled people who were overweight or obese but did not have diabetes (a much broader population than most GLP-1 trials) and still showed a 20% reduction in major cardiovascular events. That's a real differentiator for semaglutide that most comparison sites don't explain clearly.

How this should shape which provider you choose

Every trial above tested an FDA-approved branded medication (Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound, Mounjaro, or Rybelsus) at a specific, controlled dose. None of them tested a compounded formulation. That matters because a growing share of telehealth GLP-1 providers dispense compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide, made by a compounding pharmacy rather than the original manufacturer, usually at a lower cash price.

We do not claim compounded products are "clinically equivalent" to the trial-tested branded product: that specific claim is what's currently drawing FDA and legal scrutiny industry-wide, including active litigation between GLP-1 manufacturers and telehealth providers over how compounded products are marketed. The active ingredient may be the same; the trial evidence above applies to the branded product that was actually studied, not to a compounded version of it.

A separate warning specific to retatrutide: unlike semaglutide and tirzepatide, retatrutide has never been FDA-approved in any form, so there is no legal compounded version of it either: the FDA has stated this directly and issued warning letters over "compounded retatrutide" sales in September 2025. If any provider offers retatrutide, that is a signal to walk away, not a pricing tier: see the pipeline section above.

Frequently asked questions

What is the newest weight-loss drug currently in trials?

Retatrutide, Eli Lilly's triple GLP-1/GIP/glucagon agonist, is the furthest along of the drugs still in trials. Its Phase 3 TRIUMPH-1 results (announced May 2026, n=2,339) showed up to 28.3% average weight loss at 80 weeks on the 12mg dose. It is not FDA-approved and is not legally available for purchase in any form, including compounded.

When will retatrutide be approved?

No confirmed approval date exists. Eli Lilly has indicated it plans to file for FDA approval (a Biologics License Application) in Q1 2027, which would put a realistic approval window in late 2027 to 2028, assuming a standard review. Several TRIUMPH program trials are still reporting results throughout 2026.

Can I buy retatrutide online in the USA?

No, not legally, in any form. Retatrutide has never been FDA-approved, so it cannot legally be sold as a prescription drug and cannot legally be compounded either: the FDA has stated directly that retatrutide cannot be used in compounding under federal law, and issued warning letters in September 2025 to websites selling compounded retatrutide. Anything marketed online as "retatrutide peptide" outside of a clinical trial is not a legitimate, regulated medication.

Is retatrutide better than semaglutide (Ozempic)?

On average weight loss, retatrutide's Phase 3 results (up to 28.3% at 80 weeks) are higher than semaglutide's STEP 1 result (14.9% at 68 weeks), similar to how tirzepatide already outperforms semaglutide on that single measure. But retatrutide is not FDA-approved, has no long-term safety data outside trials, and has no cardiovascular-outcomes trial published yet, unlike semaglutide's SELECT trial. "Better" depends on whether you're optimizing for maximum average weight loss specifically or for the depth of safety evidence, and only one of the two is available today.

What is the new weight loss pill coming out in 2026?

Two oral GLP-1 pills are already FDA-approved and available in 2026, not just "coming out": oral semaglutide 25mg (branded as the Wegovy pill, approved December 2025) and orforglipron (branded Foundayo, approved April 1, 2026, the first oral GLP-1 with no food or water timing restrictions). Both are taken as a daily tablet rather than a weekly injection.

Why are people stopping Ozempic?

The two most commonly cited reasons are cost and gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation). The trial evidence on what happens next is clear: STEP 4 found that people who stopped semaglutide regained about 6.9% of their body weight over the following 28 weeks, and broader follow-up data shows many people who stop eventually regain roughly two-thirds of the weight they lost within a year. A meaningful share of people who stop later restart treatment.

What is the cheapest GLP-1 drug I can buy without insurance?

Among FDA-approved options, self-pay list prices as of 2026 run from about $149/month for the lowest dose of Foundayo (orforglipron) or the oral Wegovy pill, up to $995 to over $1,600/month for injectable Wegovy or Zepbound at retail without a discount. Zepbound single-dose vials sold directly by Eli Lilly for self-pay patients run $299 to $449/month. Compounded semaglutide from an FDA-registered pharmacy is often advertised even lower, around $200 to $400/month, but is not the FDA-approved, trial-tested product.

What is the absolute best weight loss pill?

There isn't a single universal answer: it depends on what you're optimizing for. For maximum studied weight loss in an oral option, orforglipron (Foundayo) at 36mg showed 11.2% average loss at 72 weeks. For the most real-world evidence behind an oral GLP-1, oral semaglutide (the Wegovy pill) has a longer track record. Neither pill has matched the weight loss seen with injectable tirzepatide or semaglutide at their highest doses.
