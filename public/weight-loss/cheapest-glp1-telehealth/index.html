What this means in plain language

Cheapest and highest-scored now overlap, but the number is introductory

Ro, CitedRx's highest-scored provider overall (9.3/10), also now publishes the lowest first-month price on this page ($39). But that's a one-time rate: Ro's ongoing cost is $149/mo, with medication billed separately, which lands it mid-pack once the intro period ends. The next-cheapest ongoing prices, Found, Embody, and AltRx, score 8.3, 3.8, and 4.5 respectively: a wide spread. A low starting price doesn't tell you whether you're getting the FDA-approved branded product the major trials actually tested, or a compounded version that isn't.

The cheapest tier isn't always the price you'll actually pay

Several of the lowest prices on this list are a floor or an introductory rate, not a flat ongoing price: Ro's $39 is a first-month-only rate that rises to $149/mo afterward, Found's range starts at $49/mo but can run to $99/mo depending on your prescribed tier, and Medvi's $179 is specifically a "first month" price. Confirm what you'd actually pay in month two and beyond before enrolling anywhere based on a headline number.

"No published price" isn't automatically a red flag

LifeMD, WeightWatchers Clinic, and Calibrate don't publish one price because their cost structure depends on your specific insurance situation and prescribed medication. All three still score well on CitedRx's methodology (9.1, 9.0, and 5.3 respectively): the absence of a sticker price reflects a plan-based model, not necessarily a less transparent one.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest GLP-1 telehealth provider?

By lowest published number, Ro's new $39 first-month price is the cheapest on this page, though it's an introductory rate that rises to $149/mo afterward (medication billed separately). Among providers with a stable ongoing price, Found's lowest tier starts around $49/mo, followed by Embody at $69/mo and AltRx at $89/mo. Starting price isn't the same as your actual cost, and it isn't the same as CitedRx Score: Embody scores 3.8/10 and AltRx 4.5/10 (with a regulatory deduction), so the cheapest option by sticker price is not automatically the best evidence-based choice.

Why do some providers not have a single starting price?

LifeMD, WeightWatchers Clinic, and Calibrate price by individual plan rather than publishing one headline number, usually because cost depends heavily on which medication and insurance situation applies to you. That's not necessarily worse: it just means you can't compare their price to a flat rate without contacting them directly.

Is the cheapest GLP-1 provider the best one?

Not necessarily. The lowest-priced providers on this page are mostly compounded-primary, meaning the clinical trial evidence behind FDA-approved GLP-1s doesn't directly apply to what they prescribe, and several carry real regulatory or litigation notes. Price is one input; see each provider's full CitedRx Score for the complete picture.
