Frequently asked questions

What is the best GLP-1 telehealth provider overall?

Based on CitedRx's scoring, Ro, LifeMD, and WeightWatchers Clinic score highest overall (9.0–9.3/10) because they prescribe predominantly FDA-approved branded GLP-1 medication and offer dedicated insurance/prior-authorization support. But "best overall" depends on your priority: someone who wants the lowest cash price may reasonably choose a lower-scored compounded provider instead.

How is the CitedRx Score calculated?

The CitedRx Score (out of 10) weighs five factors: how directly clinical trial evidence applies to what's actually prescribed (4 points), independent third-party rating (2 points), insurance and prior-authorization support (2 points), clinical support model such as coaching or monitoring (1 point), and regulatory standing, including any active FDA warning letters or unresolved litigation (1 point). See "How we scored these" above for the full breakdown.

What's the difference between compounded and brand-name GLP-1 medication?

Brand-name GLP-1s (Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound, Mounjaro) are FDA-approved products manufactured by Novo Nordisk or Eli Lilly and are the exact formulations tested in clinical trials. Compounded GLP-1s use the same active ingredient but are prepared by a compounding pharmacy rather than the original manufacturer, usually at a lower cash price: they are not individually FDA-reviewed for safety, effectiveness, or quality, and clinical trial data does not directly apply to them.

Is compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide safe?

Reputable compounding pharmacies operate under FDA-registered 503A or 503B facilities and use FDA-registered active-ingredient suppliers, but compounded products themselves are not FDA-reviewed for safety, effectiveness, or quality the way brand-name Wegovy or Zepbound are. Several providers in this category have received FDA warning letters over how compounded GLP-1s are marketed: check a provider's current regulatory standing before enrolling.

Will my insurance cover a GLP-1 prescribed through telehealth?

Sometimes, depending on your plan and diagnosis. Providers with a dedicated prior-authorization team (Ro, LifeMD, WeightWatchers Clinic, Found) tend to secure coverage for brand-name medication faster than cash-pay compounded providers, most of which offer little to no insurance support.

How much does GLP-1 weight loss telehealth cost per month?

Cash-pay compounded programs typically run $69–$350 per month depending on medication, dose, and plan length, often cheaper with a longer prepaid commitment. Brand-name GLP-1 access through insurance coordination can cost far less out-of-pocket if a prior authorization is approved, or over $1,000/month without coverage.

What happens if I stop taking a GLP-1 medication?

Clinical trial data (the STEP 4 trial) shows that people who stop a GLP-1 after losing weight tend to regain a meaningful portion of it within months, while those who continue keep losing. This is documented in a randomized trial, not speculation: see our research page for the specific figures.

Can I switch GLP-1 telehealth providers?

Generally yes: most providers don't require an exclusive long-term contract, though some prepaid plans (3, 6, or 12 months) are non-refundable once shipped. Confirm a provider's cancellation and refund policy before enrolling, especially on longer prepaid plans.

Are online GLP-1 telehealth prescriptions legitimate?

Yes, when the provider uses licensed U.S. clinicians and FDA-registered pharmacies: telehealth prescribing is legal nationwide. Legitimacy varies by provider, though: check for licensed-clinician review, transparent pharmacy sourcing, and any FDA warning letters or lawsuits before enrolling.

Which GLP-1 telehealth providers have had FDA warning letters or lawsuits?

As of this page's last review, Medvi received an FDA warning letter (February 2026) over labeling that implied it was the compounding pharmacy rather than a telehealth coordinator, and altRx's parent company Trinity Healthcare Supply received an FDA warning letter (June 2026) over website claims and labeling. Henry Meds faces active, unresolved litigation from Eli Lilly over compounded-tirzepatide marketing. This context factors into each provider's CitedRx Score above.

How often is this page updated?

GLP-1 pricing, provider lineups, and regulatory status change frequently. This page was last reviewed in September 2026; check the provider's own site for current pricing and offerings before enrolling.
