Thinking About a GLP-1? Check This Before You Pay $1,000+ Out of Pocket

The 4 mistakes people make when trying to get a GLP-1 like Zepbound or Wegovy, and the online program built to help you avoid them.

By the Trending Health team · Updated October 2026 · Sponsored by Found

If you've priced a GLP-1 recently, you know the feeling. You've read about the results, you've maybe watched a friend or coworker try one, and then you see the number: over $1,000 a month at list price for the brand-name shots.

So a lot of people stop right there. Some assume their insurance will never pay. Some start the approval process and give up halfway. Others grab the cheapest thing they can find online without asking what's actually in it.

About 1 in 8 U.S. adults say they've already taken a GLP-1.1 Here's what the ones who got it right tend to do differently.

Mistake #1: Assuming your insurance will say no

Coverage for weight-loss GLP-1s is all over the map. Some plans exclude them entirely. But many plans do cover medications like Zepbound or Wegovy for weight management, and the catch is that they want paperwork first.

That paperwork is called a prior authorization: your prescriber has to document why you qualify before the insurer agrees to pay. People who never check never find out whether they're covered.

Mistake #2: Giving up on the paperwork

This is where most people stall. Prior authorizations mean forms, medical history, back-and-forth with the insurer, and sometimes a resubmission. If your prescriber's office is busy, it can drag on for weeks or never get finished at all.

The question for a lot of people isn't whether GLP-1s work. It's whether anyone will do the paperwork to get one covered.

This is the problem one online program, Found, is built around. Insured members get a dedicated prior-authorization team that works with their insurer on coverage for brand-name GLP-1 medication. You don't have to chase the forms yourself.

Mistake #4: Not asking which medication you're getting

Many online programs sell compounded GLP-1s: versions mixed by pharmacies, not the brand-name drug. They cost less, but compounded medications are not FDA-approved, and the trial results above were measured on the brand-name versions.

That doesn't make compounded wrong for everyone. It does mean you should know which one you're getting.
